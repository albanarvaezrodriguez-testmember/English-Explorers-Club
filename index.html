Podcast Post #01

Listen and Discover: Storynory, Free Audio Stories for Kids

What is it?

Storynory is a free online platform that offers a large collection of audio stories for children, including classic fairy tales, myths, original stories, and educational narratives. All stories are narrated by professional voice actors using clear, expressive, and well-paced English, making them highly suitable for non-native learners at upper primary level.

Educational Value

Listening to stories read aloud is one of the most natural ways to acquire a language. Storynory exposes students to a rich variety of vocabulary, sentence structures, and narrative conventions in an entertaining context. The combination of audio and written transcripts on the website allows students to follow along with the text, reinforcing both listening and reading skills at the same time.

How to Use it in Class

Teachers can choose a story that connects with a current topic or reading unit and play it as a shared listening activity. Students can follow the transcript on screen, pause to discuss new vocabulary, and then retell the story in their own words either orally or in writing. For homework, students can choose their own story and prepare a short summary to share with the class.

Benefits and Challenges

Storynory is completely free, requires no registration, and works on any device with internet access. The wide range of stories ensures that there is always something suitable for different interests and reading levels. One possible challenge is that some stories contain slightly archaic vocabulary from classic tales, which may require additional teacher support to explain in context.

Role-Playing Activity Post #02

Real Conversations in English: Role-Play Scenarios by ESL Kids World

What is it?

ESL Kids World offers a collection of printable and digital role-play cards and scenario scripts designed specifically for young English language learners. The activities cover everyday situations such as ordering food at a café, buying tickets at a cinema, calling a friend to make plans, or checking in at a hotel — all adapted to a level appropriate for 5th and 6th grade students.

Educational Value

Communicative competence is one of the core goals of language learning, and role-playing is one of the most effective ways to develop it. These activities push students to produce spontaneous language in a guided context, practising functional expressions, question forms, and polite register. Working in pairs or small groups also builds confidence and reduces anxiety around speaking in a foreign language.

How to Use it in Class

The teacher selects a scenario relevant to the current unit and distributes the role cards to pairs of students. After a short preparation time, pairs perform their dialogue in front of the class or record themselves on a tablet. The activity can be extended by asking students to create their own version of the scenario with different vocabulary or a surprise twist in the plot.

Benefits and Challenges

This resource is flexible, easy to adapt, and does not require any technology beyond a printed sheet or a screen. It suits different proficiency levels within the same class. The main challenge is classroom management during group work — setting clear time limits and performance expectations helps keep activities focused and productive.

Picture Dictionary Post #03

Words Come Alive: Wordsmyth Children's Dictionary with Images

What is it?

Wordsmyth is a free online dictionary platform that includes a dedicated Children's Dictionary level, featuring simple and clear definitions, example sentences, audio pronunciation, and thematic illustrations. It is specifically designed for young learners and presents vocabulary in a visually engaging and age-appropriate format, avoiding the overly complex explanations found in standard dictionaries.

Educational Value

Building a strong vocabulary is essential for all four language skills, and having access to a dictionary that includes visual support makes word learning far more effective for this age group. Wordsmyth not only defines words but also provides audio pronunciation, which is particularly valuable for EFL learners who may not be confident about how to say a new word correctly. The example sentences help students understand words in context rather than in isolation.

How to Use it in Class

Teachers can use Wordsmyth as a reference tool during reading activities, asking students to look up unknown words and write their own example sentences. A more creative task is a "word of the day" routine: each student selects a word from the dictionary, learns its pronunciation, and presents it to the class with an original sentence and a drawing. This activity builds ownership over vocabulary learning.

Benefits and Challenges

Wordsmyth is free, accessible from any device, and offers three difficulty levels, making it easy to differentiate within the same classroom. Audio support for pronunciation is a significant advantage over printed dictionaries. One limitation is that internet access is required, so teachers should plan an offline alternative for sessions with unreliable connectivity.

Digital Storytelling Post #04

Tell Your Story: Book Creator for Schools

What is it?

Book Creator is a digital publishing tool that allows students to design and write their own illustrated e-books directly in the browser, without needing to install any software. Students can combine text, drawings, photographs, audio recordings, and video clips to produce multimodal stories in English. The platform offers a free plan for teachers that includes up to 40 books per class.

Educational Value

Creating a personal book in a foreign language is a deeply meaningful language task. Students must plan their narrative, choose vocabulary carefully, write with an awareness of their audience, and edit their own work. The multimodal nature of Book Creator also allows students with different strengths to contribute in different ways — a student who struggles with writing may shine through illustration or audio narration, making the activity genuinely inclusive.

How to Use it in Class

The teacher sets up a class library and assigns a creative brief — for example, writing a story about an imaginary adventure, a biography of a historical figure, or a guide to their hometown in English. Students work on their books over several sessions, going through a full writing process: drafting, peer feedback, revision, and final publication. Finished books can be shared with families or other classes.

Benefits and Challenges

Book Creator is intuitive enough for 10–12 year olds to use independently after a short introduction. The sense of producing a real, shareable product is highly motivating and gives writing a genuine purpose. The main challenge is time — a quality book project requires several sessions, so it works best as a longer project rather than a one-off activity.

Extra Resource 1 Post #05

Play and Learn: Quizlet Live for Vocabulary Practice

What is it?

Quizlet is a widely used digital flashcard and study platform that allows teachers to create sets of vocabulary cards which students can then practise through a variety of game modes. The Quizlet Live feature turns vocabulary revision into a real-time collaborative classroom game where students are divided into random teams and must work together to match words and definitions correctly.

Educational Value

Research consistently shows that spaced repetition and active retrieval are among the most effective strategies for vocabulary retention. Quizlet is built around these principles, offering multiple study modes — flashcards, written recall, matching games, and timed tests — that target the same vocabulary from different angles. The collaborative element of Quizlet Live adds a social dimension that encourages peer learning and communication in English.

How to Use it in Class

Teachers create a vocabulary set linked to the current unit — for example, animals, daily routines, or describing people — and share the access code with students. For independent practice, students can use the flashcard and test modes at home. In the classroom, Quizlet Live works as an energising team activity that reviews vocabulary in a competitive but cooperative context, as a wrong answer affects the whole team and encourages discussion.

Benefits and Challenges

Quizlet is available on all devices and offers a generous free plan. The ability to add images and audio to flashcard sets makes it particularly effective for EFL learners. One challenge is that the free version has some limitations on teacher features, and the most interactive classroom modes work best when every student has their own device.

Extra Resource 2 Post #06

Watch and Understand: English with Fun — Comprehensible Input Videos

What is it?

Comprehensible Input videos are short educational clips produced specifically for language learners, in which the content is delivered in English at a controlled pace and with visual support — images, gestures, and on-screen text — that helps students understand the meaning even without knowing every word. Channels such as Dreaming Spanish (English version) and English with Lucy for Beginners follow this approach, making them ideal for upper primary EFL students.

Educational Value

The comprehensible input theory, developed by linguist Stephen Krashen, proposes that language is acquired most effectively when learners are exposed to content that is slightly beyond their current level but still understandable through context. These videos apply this principle directly, offering natural English in a format that does not feel like a lesson but still promotes genuine acquisition of vocabulary, grammar patterns, and pronunciation.

How to Use it in Class

Teachers can select a short video (5–10 minutes) connected to a topic being studied — nature, history, science, or daily life — and use it as a listening and viewing activity. Students watch once without stopping, then watch again pausing at key moments to discuss or take notes. A follow-up task — a short written summary, a vocabulary list, or a discussion question — consolidates the input and moves students towards output.

Benefits and Challenges

This type of resource exposes students to authentic, natural English in a low-anxiety format, which is particularly beneficial for learners who feel nervous about speaking. Videos are highly engaging for this age group and connect language learning to real-world knowledge. The main challenge is selecting age-appropriate content carefully, as YouTube channels vary widely in quality and suitability for primary school contexts.
